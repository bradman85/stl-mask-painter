Guide

Selection?

Select the part of the model the stencil should expose. Build stencil is built from this selection and nothing else — there is nothing to paint.

The selection is one thing, shared by every tool: take the head with Part, then take the eyes back off it with Select faces a few triangles at a time, and Build stencil uses what is left. Switching tools never clears it, because a selection is not a tool's private state.

What is selected is tinted amber, and — while Select faces is the tool in hand — the triangle under the cursor is outlined white, so a click on a triangle is judged before it happens. Part outlines nothing: it takes the whole panel the click lands on, which the status bar names. Click to select and again to unselect, Shift-click adds, Alt-click takes one off, Ctrl+A takes the lot, Esc clears it.

Seams & panels?

Move the slider to cut the model into parts.

The Part tool selects the whole panel under the cursor — head, torso, a limb — so the window is exactly that panel. A thinner seam angle cuts the model into more, smaller panels; a click near a thin panel offers the neighbours in the list below.

Windows?

Every separate group of selected faces is one window in the built stencil — one hole. Two groups that do not touch become two windows, and if they are far apart the print splits into two pieces. These two buttons find and drop the ones you did not mean.

Tint & view?

The three chips in the top-right corner of the viewport are the switches for the layers that can hide one another — the built Stencil, the Model under it and the amber Selection tint — and they report each layer's state. The checkboxes below are the same switches.

Model?

Right-drag orbit · middle-drag pan · wheel zoom · hold Space to orbit with the left button. Selecting never moves the model; the mask, the panels and every export keep the file's own coordinates.

Stencil (STL)?

The selected area becomes a window in a one-piece shell that sits on the model. Hold the printed mask on the figure and only the selected area is exposed.

Reach is where the mask material starts. With widening on it grows by itself until the mask is a single piece — good for a window that came out in several groups, but it can spread over the whole figure. Turn it off to keep a band around the window (builds much faster). Standoff is the gap the mask leaves over the model so it can be slid into place; the build reports the gap it actually achieved. Numbers are in model units (mm for a millimetre model).

Export / import?

Save selection (JSON) is the session file: the window you have selected, plus the build settings. Load the same model later, Open selection (JSON)…, and carry on adding to it — the PLY is only the selected shell. A mask saved by an older version of this app still opens: it carried several paint colours, and all of them come back as the one selection.
