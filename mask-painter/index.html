Flood fill stops where the surface turns more than the seam angle below.

Drag a rectangle over the model: every vertex under it that faces you is painted with the current feature, as one undo step. Alt-drag (or Ctrl-drag) releases paint instead, Esc cancels the drag, and with Mirror across X on the rectangle is taken on the other side of the plane too.

Pick faces and take them out of the model. A file often holds more than one model — a figure plus a base, a duplicate, a stray blob — and the bed sits under the lowest point of any of them, so an unwanted one leaves the part you want hanging in the air. Click the model under the cursor (again to unpick), pick single faces instead, or Ctrl+A for the lot and click off the few you want to keep. This tool paints nothing.

Right-drag orbit · middle-drag pan · wheel zoom · hold Space to orbit with the left button.

Paint stencil (STL)?

The painted area becomes a window in a one-piece shell that sits on the model. Hold the printed mask on the figure and only the painted area is exposed.

Reach is where the mask material starts. With widening on it grows by itself until the mask is a single piece — good for a window you painted by hand, but it can spread over the whole figure. Turn it off to keep a band around the window (builds much faster). Standoff is the gap the mask leaves over the model so it can be slid into place; the build reports the gap it actually achieved. Numbers are in model units (mm for a millimetre model).

Export / import?

Save mask (JSON) is the session file: the windows you marked up, plus the palette and the build settings. Load the same model later, Open mask (JSON)…, and carry on adding windows — the PLY is only the painted shell, in per-vertex colours.
